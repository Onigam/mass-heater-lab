# The 3D worlds

Four worlds built from `src/three/dims.ts` (metres, the one source, every constant tagged `reported`, `derived` or `estimate`; what the simulation owns is imported from `MACHINE`, and `dims.test.ts` holds that nothing is retyped). `src/three/index.ts` exports `createHomesteadScene`, `createLivingRoomScene`, `createFireCoreScene`, `createBenchSectionScene`, all `lazyModule`s, and `WORLD` (the `SceneDef` fields of each). Ids as frozen in `docs/research.md` §4: `homestead` 11, `living-room` 18, `fire-core` 14, `bench-section` 13, 56 in all, held to `src/data/ids.ts` and to a sheet each by `worlds.test.ts`. No text in any texture, no canvas, no file. Overall sizes only: no dimension is drawn in any world, and neither dial carries a figure. No closing damper: a test holds that the word is not in the source.

## Frames

**Room frame.** Y up from the slab's top, x = z = 0 at the room's centre; the back wall at z = −2.5, the gable at x = +3, the stone wall at x = −3, the front at z = +2.5. `living-room` and `fire-core` are drawn in it directly. **Core frame:** riser's axis at x = z = 0, y on the slab; one translation (`CORE.x`, `CORE.z`) puts it in the room, with no rotation, so that the feed is at −x, the gases leave at +x, and the flame reads from left to right. `homestead` moves the house and the chimney by `HOME.at`. `bench-section` has a frame of its own and takes the same profile, the same duct's axes and the same chimney (moved).

The story reads from left to right along the back wall: feed, tunnel, drum, manifold, bench, then towards the viewer along the gable, tee, chimney.

Each id lives in one world, so a world shows the pieces of another as scenery. `followers.ts` ties such a piece to a part: it hides with it, moves with its explode if it says so, and a shell goes to the ghost with it, so that X-ray and isolate see through it. `machine.test.ts` holds the shared pieces within 3 mm across the worlds, by measuring them.

**Choices the geometry forced.** The tunnel's length is measured from the feed's near face to the riser's axis (0.40 m), or the feed's brick would go through the drum. The drum is notched over the tunnel, as real builds do: it stands on the ring on the far side (`plinthHeight`, 0.16 m) and on the tunnel's roof on the near side. The fall is therefore a horseshoe under the drum, around to the manifold. The duct's axis is 0.40 m behind the core's plane: the riser's axis stands away from the back wall by the drum's radius and some air. The manifold's first 0.28 m count in the first leg, which is 4.8 m from the box's centre to the corner.

**Attached and resting** (`machine.test.ts`): sticks' feet on the tunnel's floor, lid on the rim, sleeve on the tunnel's roof, liner against insulation, gap = model's, duct from the manifold's mouth into the tee, flue on the tee, cap on the flue, brackets and crossing to the gable, dial on the flue's skin, spacers from the wall to the sheet, alarms on the wall and the ledger, probe from its dial into the earth, the corner's duct to the tee, the cleanout to the duct. Lowest point of each heavy body against its support, measured on its meshes.

**Not used:** `phase` (but for the lid), `firePower`, `woodToday`, `airFlow` (`gasFlow` is), `riserSpeed`, `ductSpeed`, `chimneySpeed` (each dot's speed comes from `gasFlow` over the local density and section, which is the same physics at every point of the path), `lambda`, `o2`, `pathS` (the path's lengths are the 3D's own), `afterDrumT`, `exhaustT`, `dewPointT`, `condensate`, `condensing` (no drip at the drain: a jar with no level is what is there), `draft*`, `lidPressure`, `brickT`, `sleeveT`, `flueOutT`, `seatHotT`, `seatColdT`, `roomT`, the power figures, the CO figures, `alarms`, `fireCount`, `burning` but for the embers, `flowDirection` (the sign of `gasFlow` says it), `benchPeakHour`.

**What the drawn machine does not follow: a weak point for the reviewer.** The sliders of `bore`, `riserHeight`, `topGap`, `ductLength` and `chimneyTop` change the model's numbers and not the drawn shape: a 4.5 m duct still draws 9 m, with the model's segments spread along it. The presets and the two bench scenarios are honest in numbers and in colours, not in shape.

## Thermal view

`thermal.ts`. One ramp, the model's (`THERMAL_RAMP`, `heatRampGlsl`, 15 to 65 °C), fixed: a colour means the same at any hour and in every world. Any material reads a number or a small half-float field (`Field`) through the attribute `aHeatUv`; part of the colour stays emissive, so a shadow never hides a temperature, and lines every 5 K let the eye read a nearly flat field. The switch fades in half a second and the lights go down with it.

- **`living-room`:** everything takes the colours; the bench reads its seat and front by segment, the drum its three values, the chimney, the stone wall, the floor and the walls theirs. With the Section tool the long section of the bench shows the heat inside, along the whole run.
- **`bench-section`:** the cut faces carry the map at all times, the stones and the earth still showing under it (`min` in the patch). The switch turns the rest of the world to the ramp too.
- **`fire-core` and `homestead`:** no thermal view. On a ramp of 15 to 65 °C the core is one colour and the outside of a well-insulated house another: it would teach nothing.

**How it is reached:** (1) the button **Vue thermique / Thermal view** in the Simulation panel, right under the hour slider (`src/panels/sim.ts`): it is a view, not a setting, so a scenario's lesson stays; (2) the same button on the sheets of the drum, the bench, the backrest, the stone wall, the cob casing and the two runs (`partActions` in `src/lab.ts`), from Explore; (3) for a film, `sim.set({ thermalView: true })`. The kit's toolbar has no slot for it (`docs/kit-requests.md`, 11). The scale is a bar on a stand with two of the kit's labels (20 and 60 °C), shown only with the view in `living-room` and always in `bench-section`.

**What shows through.** The stage has no top: a wall or the ceiling steps aside when it stands between the eye and the room. With the thermal view in `living-room` the spruces outside would stand lit in the night through the room, and they say nothing (they are under the ramp), so they go at half the fade; the windows then show the night. `bench-section` has no outside: its background is the plaster's tone above the room's back, the night with the thermal view.

**An honest limit.** The seat swings from 24.9 to 32 °C (physics, §What the model finds), so on this ramp the bench's skin moves from one teal to another: the « aha » is inside it. It reads on the cut faces and in the long section, where the earth above the duct reaches 64 °C at 20:45, and the cell on its wall's side 74 °C. The owner may want to judge the ramp on the surface: it is the model's, and I did not touch it.

## Section

`section.ts`. **A plane that follows the gases**, one surface in three steps: through the riser's axis on the core, stepping back just past the drum's ring to the duct's axis, along the first leg, then along the second (the corner untouched) and up the chimney. The casing is drawn twice with different planes to make the step. Clipping planes never change in number, and off moves them far away, so pressing the tool compiles nothing. Caps are flat rectangles in unclipped materials, carrying the field. On the viewer's side of the plane the sticks are not drawn.

- `living-room`: a tool. Section and X-ray both turn on the gases and hide the furniture in X-ray; the X-ray shell is the drum (with the casing), the bench, the backrest, the chimney's skin, the shield, what stands on them.
- `fire-core`: always on, no tool.
- `bench-section`: the faces are always cut; the tool opens the two slices lengthwise on the duct's axis, showing the gases, the joint and the heat along them.
- `homestead`: none.

Selecting the manifold opens the casing around it, as otherwise only its outline would show through the earth. **Weak point for the reviewer:** seen from the front in `living-room` the manifold stands hidden behind the drum; it is reached by selecting it, by X-ray or by Section.

## Fire

`fire/flame.ts`: ribbons that always face the camera, following the path of the gases from the sticks' feet, along the tunnel and up the riser's foot; with a negative length they stand up the feed and out of its mouth. A turbulent pattern scrolls along them towards the tip. Deep orange at the edge, yellow in the body, a touch of blue at the very foot. Additive with a little cover, in HDR. No texture, no random number, a pure function of `flameClock`: replaying a frame gives the same picture. `glowOf`: the liner shows a glow only over about 480 °C. In the default the liner reads about 260 °C at 9:00: the fire's picture does not lie about it.

## How a part is framed

Every part has a `FRAMING` in its world's `scene.ts`: a `viewDir`, so that a click or the voice's single `[[frame:id]]` reads the part from the same side whatever the camera was doing, and a `frame` where the whole box would keep the camera too far or point at the wrong place (`cob-bench` at about 20 m became about 10 m; `duct-joint` on one joint rather than the box of five; `downdraft-annulus` on the left of the drum, where its label stands). Directions were chosen by rendering each part with and without itself from a grid of directions (5 to 6 elevations, 7 to 12 bearings), preferring the world's home side when it does nearly as well. The camera turned to 6 directions then click, as the review measured it: 0 of 324 cases under 10 % of the part (54 parts, with `feed-lid` and `drum-seal`; 92 of 312 before), the lowest `floor-barrier` at 22 % and `riser-sleeve` at 28 %, thin in section. `top-gap` and `downdraft-annulus` are gases and draw next to nothing, as before; the review left them out too.

Two frames of the voice name two parts and so keep the direction they arrive with (the kit's rule): `co-alarm,smoke-alarm` comes after `chimney-flue`, which is therefore looked at from 4° above its target, under the ledger the smoke alarm hangs from; `duct-hot-run,duct-cold-run` comes after the home view. Do not change those directions without looking at what follows.

Labels: a name runs to the right of its point, so the anchors stand to the left of what they name, and the home of `bench-section` is turned by 19° to bring the pebbles and the tee in from the edge. `labels.test.ts` holds all four sides, the toolbar and the sheet at the five sizes the review measured.

## For the lead

`WORLD[id]` spreads into each `SceneDef`. `src/lab.ts` holds the four worlds, their overviews, the `partLinks` (`house-shell` → `living-room`, `steel-drum` → `fire-core`, `cob-bench` → `bench-section`), both guides, the plans' groups and the Sources panel. A landscape needs two small things the kit keeps for a world called `site`: the camera's planes (`scene.onBeforeRender` in `homestead/scene.ts`) and the lowest camera (`onFrame` in `src/lab.ts`); `docs/kit-requests.md` 10 asks for the real thing.

Film notes: `update` is the only thing that moves a world, and `dt` = 0 moves nothing. Every motion takes `view.slowMo`. Boxes that do not stay put: `fuel-sticks` shrinks with `feedLevel`, `feed-lid` moves with the cover, `cordwood-stack` shrinks with the season. Frame those on a neighbour if a shot must hold still. The kit's labels are the only words: none is in a texture.
