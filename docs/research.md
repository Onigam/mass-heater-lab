> **Tags.** _reported_ = said by a source. _derived_ = computed from reported figures, formula given. _assumption_ = a choice we make. _estimate_ = no source, reasoning given.

## 0. Summary and the plan against the bar

### 0.1 The machine

**A 200 mm (8-inch) J-tube rocket mass heater: a vertical feed, a firebrick burn tunnel, an insulated heat riser under a 200 L steel drum, then about 9 m of duct buried in about 3 t of cob, the bench, and a chimney through the roof, in the living room of a small well-insulated house.** It burns two sessions a day of about 2.5 h each, about 12.5 kg of wood. All of it is a **generic reconstruction of a class of machine**, never anyone's product and never a plan.

**Why this one.** It is the class of the videos: nine of the twelve builds of the roadmap's main video are 8-inch J-tubes, all twelve are J-tubes, half are cob (K2); it is what the French video shows (K3), and the 1.8-million-view film of a builders' house in January (K66). It has the most public figures for its size: the builders' burn rates and exhaust (K5 to K11), a draft code text (K13), a real gas analysis (K20), a bench read through a burn and the night after (K14). Its flame is the picture of the subject, sucked sideways into a tunnel.

### 0.2 What checking the sources changed

The roadmap's §5.5 was written from titles and needs these corrections. Each is sourced below.

1. **No source in the roadmap has a figure you can test, and one is about another machine.** The main video has **no spoken word at all**: music over time-lapses, with a closing sale of plans by the same man (S1, S9). The French video is a 4-minute introduction (S2). **The « tuto » is not a mass heater: it is a small rocket stove made of tins and earth, with no drum, no bench and no flue in what I saw, burning cardboard, tested outdoors (S3)**. Worse, its author plans to use it in « mon fourgon » and says it can heat « n'importe quelle pièce » (≈1:35, ≈12:00). **A lab about carbon monoxide must not link that.** Oxalis warned of the confusion in 2016: the name « a été détourné » for low-consumption cookers (S23 p. 11). **S3 leaves the sources** (Q7). Added: **the most-watched film of this machine, in a real house in January, with its wood on camera (S51), proposed as the main video**; a builder's retrospective of last month (S4), four builders' measurements (S11, S17, S18, S21), a French manual free to reuse (S23), a Finnish university's measurements (S28), a Swiss federal note (S30), the WHO's guideline (S34).
2. **« Two fires a day » is true of batch boxes and masonry stoves, not literally of this machine.** A J-tube burn is a tended session of 2 to 4 hours at 1 to 2 loads an hour (K5, one builder). The film's question stands, and the lab says what a « fire » is: a morning and an evening at the feed, not one armful and walk away. The literal table « mean power, two fires a day » exists, and it is for batch boxes (K28).
3. **« 80 to 90 % less wood » does not balance on energy.** Even granting a 90 % burn, from a stove really run at 35 % it saves 61 %, not 90 % (K55). The most-quoted comparison, 0.6 cords a year in a 2,000 sq ft Montana house of average insulation, needs 3.9 to 8 times more useful heat than it can deliver at the source's own figures (K54), and it is a stated estimate, not a measure (S10). The honest answer to the film's question is **insulation first**: two fires deliver about 1.7 kW day and night, so a house that loses more than that needs more fires (K51 to K53). The lab says so plainly (Q6).
4. **I found no independent laboratory test of a classic cob J-tube, anywhere.** Builders say so themselves (S11, S25, S26). The one peer-reviewed paper is a computer model behind a paywall (S12). The only third-party lab test found is a commercial pellet-fed unit, 68.5 to 79.7 % on higher heating value, not 90 % (K23). Every « efficiency » from a hand-held analyser is **calculated from the flue**, and leaves out what the drum and bench do (S16, S17, S23). The lab shows ranges and says who said what, once.
5. **The roadmap's parts list is wrong for a J-tube, on one point dangerously.** There is no « porte » (a lid, `feed-lid`). And the « clapet »: a draft code says « no dampers installed that can obstruct free flow » (K13); one designer writes that a damper closed over glowing coals « can be very dangerous », and that carbon monoxide « will kill people in their sleep » (S16); another calls it a risk of toxic gas in the house (S23); one old report recommends shutting the flue and, ten pages before, says not to use a damper (S27). **No closing damper in the 3D; a scenario shows why** (§5.6, Q2). Schumack's model finds that a damper can improve efficiency (S12): that partial damper is what the lesson discusses, with the warning.
6. **The roadmap's `sim/climate` does not exist.** `lab-kit/src/sim/` holds `electric.ts`, `hydraulic.ts`, `index.ts`. Outside temperature is a small local function here (§5.5). And the thermal core has no `stackDraft` yet, nor radiation: the sand notes listed them as hooks, the code has neither (§5.8).
7. **A wrong link in the main source's own description**: its « Cool, Clean Exhaust from a Rocket Mass Heater » points to a video on splitting cedar logs (S1, checked). So the claim of a cool exhaust is not backed by what it links. Measured exhaust ranges from 32 °C to over 232 °C depending on who measures what (K10).
8. **Rejected source**: the one journal paper with « mass heater » in its title gives wood's heating value as 1,232 kJ/kg, about 12 times too low, and an efficiency of « 35 % or more » without measurement (S45). No figure taken from it.
9. **Sources kept out of the links**: search results offer pirated PDFs of both reference books (one with a « (B.O.O.K.$) » title on Zenodo, others on file hosts). I did not open them and the lab does not link them. See Q8.

### 0.5 A machine that can kill: what the lab shows and what it does not

**The rule, from the roadmap and BRIEF §8: it explains; it gives no ready-to-build plan.** Fire, carbon monoxide and a house full of sleeping people are all involved. The frame's modes stay, and change their meaning:

- **Assembly** becomes **« how a mason raises it »**: 20 steps, each saying what the step is **for**, what is checked, what goes wrong if it is skipped. **Installation** becomes **« what must be true before the first fire »**: 10 steps, from « does this house qualify » through the insurer in writing, the floor, the flue, the air, a professional's review, to the routine of a winter.
- As on the sand lab, each step's last words carry its origin: _reported_, _usual practice_ or _our reconstruction_. `torque` stays empty; `duration` only for reported totals. Each id of `living-room`, `fire-core` and `bench-section` is installed in exactly one step, so nothing floats (Mill's test).

| The lab shows                                                                                   | The lab does not show                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Overall sizes, rounded: drum, riser height, bench length and section, chimney height            | A brick-by-brick layout, a cut list, a shopping list with quantities                       |
| Ratios and clearances **as a source's words, attributed and dated**, in the sheets' notes (K13) | Those ratios as a design rule or a « safe » value; any way to reduce a clearance           |
| Materials and why, with the makers' data                                                        | A cob or refractory mix, a mortar, a curing or first-fire schedule                         |
| What goes wrong, in numbers (cold start, wet wood, leak, damper, extraction)                    | A lighting procedure as instructions. Sheets and guide say what happens, never « do this » |
| How to find out: who to ask, what standard exists, what an insurer asks                         | A statement that anything is safe, compliant or legal                                      |

Every sheet's title block carries **« Schéma explicatif, pas un plan de construction / Explanatory diagram, not a construction drawing »**. A persistent warning needs a kit feature (§7.5, Q11).

### 0.6 Sheets, lessons, spoken guide

**Sheets (A3, from `dims.ts`, estimates marked):** 1 `general-arrangement` (room plan and elevation, overall sizes only) · 2 `long-section` (the whole gas path unrolled, with model temperatures) · 3 `core-section` (feed, tunnel, riser, drum, cut; proportions with their attributed sources) · 4 `duct-plan` (the run, bends, cleanouts, tee) · 5 `bench-cross-sections` (two casings; heat map at 0, 4, 8, 12 h, model output) · 6 `materials-explained` (nomenclature: brick, insulation, earth, steel, with maker data and the reason; not a shopping list) · 7 `firing-day` (the roadmap's « schéma de flambée »: 24 h chart of feed, drum, bench, room, model output) · 8 `draft-budget` (pressure along the path, cold and hot, model output) · 9 `energy-day` (Sankey of a day: wood → drum, bench, flue, unburned; house losses) · 10 `safety-chain` (CO thresholds, clearances as attributed, the law by country, who to ask).

**Lessons:** 1 `the-fire-goes-sideways` (draft from a warm column; riser against chimney; why a cold flue smokes back) · 2 `smoke-is-fuel` (hot, air, time; dry wood; what a CO reading means; what the eye cannot judge, K20) · 3 `where-the-heat-goes` (drum now, bench later, chimney lost; a duct too short or too long) · 4 `charge-and-give-back` (**the aha**: heat crossing cob in hours, thick against thin, the seat warmest after the flame; two fires 12 h apart) · 5 `two-fires-which-house` (the balance: 1.7 kW against the house's losses, why insulation does most of the work, and which claims do not balance) · 6 `fire-co-and-the-law` (CO physics and thresholds, the damper trap, extraction fans, the insurer; when this machine is the wrong answer). Two quiz questions each.

**Spoken guide:** 56 parts × about 270 characters, about six star parts (`steel-drum`, `cob-bench`, `feed-tube`, `heat-riser`, `cob-casing`, `co-alarm`) at about 400, four worlds, an introduction: about 21,000 characters per language. Character to follow: **a breath of cold air** that enters at the feed, catches fire, climbs the riser, falls down the drum, crawls along the bench and leaves almost cool, in the Pelton manner. Anonymous as usual. Said once: reconstruction, generic machine, model figures. The CO warning is said once, on `co-alarm`, and one sentence on the feed where the gesture is risky. Written after the checkpoint, from the final sheets.

### 2.7 Conflicts between sources, and what the lab does

1. **What a « fire » is.** A J-tube session of 2–4 h (K5) against one hour's load in a batch box or a masonry stove (K29, K30). **The lab's default: two J-tube sessions of about 2.5 h, 12.5 kg a day, inside K7 and K8; the panel says it in words. A preset gives the batch load.**
2. **Exhaust temperature**, 32 to 232 °C (K10). All can be true: length, mass, firing time and where the probe sits. **Let the model find it per scenario; show the spread with attribution.** The « near room temperature » of S8 is at most a very long system near the end of a burn. Note K41: a 32–40 °C exhaust means wet condensate in the flue and little draft.
3. **Wood saved.** ÷ 2.5 at best from efficiency alone against a decent stove used well; ÷ 18 against a smouldering one (K55). **The lab shows a slider on the old stove's real efficiency, and credits insulation separately.**
4. **The damper.** « Do not use a damper in the chimney » and « shutting the flue after a three to four hour burn solves this problem » are in the same report (S27). Closed on coals it can kill (S16); a risk of toxic gas (S23); no obstructing damper (S15); 5 % open at least (S26); partial ones raise efficiency (S12); Oxalis closes the **air inlet**, as « la technique actuelle » (S23 p. 32). **No closing damper in the 3D; `feed-lid` is a lid that limits air, with its warning; the scenario gives the numbers.**
5. **Who drives the draft: the riser or the chimney?** Popular accounts give the riser the credit (S8, Wikipedia). Worked out by hand (K39), riser and drum together net a few pascals, and a warm chimney gives 8 to 29 Pa. S16 says it plainly: the chimney is the motive force. S23 reconciles them: the inner riser matters **when the stove is cold**, and much less when hot. S46's claim that a cooling drum « sucks » is the same balance. **The model finds it; lesson 1 tells it. Say so, it surprises.**
6. **The bench's role.** Oxalis calls its own bench thermally negligible (its body does the job, S23 p. 31); in a rocket heater the bench is the store (S1, S2, S14). Both true: two different machines. **Do not copy that sentence across.**
7. **Insurance in France.** « L'auto-constructeur n'est soumis à aucune norme, si ce n'est celles … de son assureur » (S23, 2016); same rules as a professional, or no payout (S33); « les assurances ne couvrent plus » (S43); all risks covered (S43, a book's sales page). No insurer's text. **The lab says: ask yours, before, in writing. It quotes none of these.**
8. **Heating value.** 4.16 (EN), 4 at 20 % moisture (S23), 4.6 (K28, « air-dried »; K37 gives that at about 10 % moisture). **Use K37 with moisture as a slider; test K28's arithmetic as stated, and report it against EN's.**
9. **Portland's code.** Adopted (S47, forum) or an advisory (S26, which says « advisory only, not a permit »)? I could reach neither the city's page nor the current text. **Call it « a draft text written for one US city's advisory committee », nothing more.**

### 2.9 Sanity checks

- **Energy.** No source's useful heat exceeds its wood, but K18's ratios do not close on an old stove used correctly (K54, K55). `compare` tests report.
- **K28 is one formula**: the load is 6 kg × (D/150)³ to rounding, and the power is load × 2 × 3.7 kWh / 24 h. A volume law, so a bigger system holds more per unit of section. His 3.7 kWh/kg delivered is about 11 % above what EN's heating value and his own 80 % would give: **use EN's in the model and report the difference**.
- **K11 and K57** agree on flow within a factor of 2, across a 6-in and an 8-in system: good enough for an eyeballed figure.
- **K5, K6, K7**: 4 h at 2.3–2.8 kg/h = 9–11 kg, the same builder consistent with herself. **K8** is one to two of her days. Two sessions of 2.5 h at 2.5 kg/h = 12.5 kg sits inside: this is the default, not a discovery.
- **K33 against K49**: a 3 t bench clears ASTM's 800 kg by mass, but **a J-tube's open feed and cob are not what ASTM's text describes** (S26).
- **S5's own numbers do not close.** It takes a labelled 75 % stove as really 59 %, and a rocket's test as 93 %. From 59 to 93 % saves 37 %, yet it says users of stoves run « very wisely » save 75 to 80 %. To save a tenth's 90 % against a 93 % burn, the old stove must really run at 9 %. Its « 20 % efficiency on average » gives 78 %: that is the honest core of the claim, **most of the saving is the old stove's misuse**. Its « 93 % » is the same as K19's calculated figure from a batch box at his own event (my inference). Its « label is allowed 16 extra percentage points » and « the law requires an exit temperature of at least 350 degrees » have no source and I could not check them: not repeated.
- **Nothing here measures a real cob J-tube's delivered heat** (§0.2, point 4). `compare` tests report; they cannot validate.

### 5.2 Combustion (`src/sim/combustion`, new, lab side)

- **Inputs:** feed rate (kg/h, K6), moisture (K37), stick size, lid. A `state.fire` with phases cold, lighting, roaring, burning down, embers; a load that decays. For the `batch` preset, one load with a burn-rate curve (K29).
- **Air:** from the draft solver (§5.3). λ = ṁ_air/(ṁ_fuel·K35).
- **Heat:** Hu(M) from K37, whose formula already counts the water's latent heat: do not subtract it twice. Gas temperature from enthalpy, at the tunnel's exit, with losses to brick.
- **Unburned:** a **regime, not a claim**. No validated model predicts ppm here. Use a small empirical map from chamber temperature, λ and moisture to a CO fraction, labelled « indicative », bracketed by K20, K19, K22 and K25; the output says **regime** first (clean, starved, cold, smouldering, too much air) and a ppm range second. A test asserts the ordering, not the values.
- **Drying and steam:** wet wood costs latent heat and lowers the chamber.

### 5.3 The gas path and the draft (`src/sim/draft`, generic parts to the core)

A chain of segments: inlet (lid, gap between sticks) → feed → tunnel → riser → gap → annulus → manifold → duct (n segments, bends) → tee → chimney → cap → outside. Each with height, section, wall temperature, gas temperature.

- **Solve ṁ each fast step** by bisection, no allocation: `Σ g·H_i·(ρ_out(z) − ρ_gas,i) = Σ (f·L/D + K_i)·½·ρ·V²` with K_i from usual tables (estimate, listed) and `tubeFlow`'s friction. Outside density at the outside air's temperature above the roof line; **room density below it**, so a cold flue in a warm room gives the **negative draft** of K39. Room depressurisation from fans adds a term.
- **Reversal is an answer**, not an error: ṁ may be negative: then room air, or smoke, flows backward. This is the cold start and the smoke-back.
- **Heat:** gases `marchFluid` through the drum annulus (with the riser-sleeve wall and drum wall), then each duct segment to the first ring of its grid. **Laminar-turbulent transition at Re 2,700 (K57): state plainly what `tubeFlow` does there; this one number decides the exhaust temperature, so report its sensitivity.** Bends add turbulence: an estimate.
- **Report, do not fit:** exhaust against K10, flows against K11, draft terms against K39, λ against K36, flue loss against K58, the **split drum, bench, flue, which no source gives**. And the direction of S12's four findings (K42): a damper and longer duct up, low chimney and small gap down. The last is a `compare` by sign only.
- **Condensation:** gas temperature against dew point (K41) per segment; flags `state.condensing`, with a count of water per day.

### 5.4 The bench

Each of about 6 duct segments gets an `xy` grid about 5 × 4 cells, cob with stones as a weighted material. Inner face from the gases; top and front through plaster to the room (K31 as a compare, a split radiative and convective link in the model); back to the wall node; bottom to slab and ground. **Run hot and cold casings from `dims.ts`** so the two thicknesses show (K13, K56). Lengthwise conduction along the bench is negligible: a test says how negligible. Node count about 120 plus core and room: `stepNetwork` handles it. Hand estimate of its stable step: the thin drum sets it, about 100 s (9.6 kJ/K against about 48 W/K, with the core's 0.5 margin), so a slow clock of 60 s a frame needs a few substeps at most; the fire's own heat rate varies faster than that, so keep the slow step short while burning.

The 3D reads, per segment, the grid's cell temperatures to colour the cut and each plaster surface for the thermal view, at a low rate.

### 5.7 The 24 h slider, the film, the tests

**The « aha », and how to keep it honest.** On `reset()`, run three days at a coarse step, offline, deterministic, no timer; keep a table of 96 frames of the last day's field. The slider `params.hour` reads the table, and the colours follow. Live play runs the slow clock from the same state. It takes a kit request: a false-colour legend in °C that says once this is false colour (the sand lab's item 8), and a time slider. The sand lab's speed labels (item 4) are wanted too. **The ramp cannot be the sand lab's**: `sand-battery-lab/src/three/heat.ts` spans 0 to 600 °C, on which a bench between 20 and 60 °C is one shade of blue. For the modeller: one fixed span of about 15 to 65 °C, as a thermal camera sets one, with drum and core saturating, and the shader and its test reused; fixed, so that a colour means the same at any hour, in both worlds.

**Film contract** (`onigam-video/docs/lab-film-contract.md`): the kit's `LabSim` with `step(dt)`, `set(patch)`, `params.slowMo`, `params.timeScale`, `params.running`. **Two clocks**: the flame, gases and sparks at `dt`; the hour, the bench and the room at `dt × params.timeScale`; with `timeScale` 0 the bench stops and the flame runs on, which is how a film holds a burning stove at 7 a.m. No timers, no wall clock, seeded randomness. **What a film asks for, flat and without the interface:** `applyPreset(id)`, `applyScenario(id)`, `reset()`, `lightFire()`, `addLoad()`, `closeLid()`, `params.hour`, `params.thermalView`, `params.feedKgH`. **Moments as numbers on `state`:** `fireCount`, `burning`, `flowDirection` (sign flips at smoke-back), `coAlarm`, `benchPeakHour`. ## 7. Safety, law and credits

**This section is for the team, not for the visitor as it stands. I am not a lawyer; each law pointer says how I reached it. Re-read before any of it is shown.**

### 7.1 Hazards, for the sheets and one warning each, where the gesture is risky

- **Carbon monoxide**: it « can't be seen or smelled » (S16); a slow fire makes much of it (K22, K25); a cold flue pushes it into the room (K39, S23); embers keep making it after the flame is gone. A closed damper or a blocked flue turns a stove into a gas leak (S16: « it will kill people in their sleep when leaked into the house »). Cracks in cob, joints in the duct and the drum's lid are the leak paths (usual reasoning, no incident data). An alarm is « indispensable » (S16) and **it is not enough**: it stays silent at levels WHO calls too high (K61).
- **Fire**: 200 °C drum and up (K29), radiation to walls (clearances in S15, attributed); chimney fire in a soot-coated flue; embers at the feed; foam or wood under the base (S23); fabric on the bench. **A J-tube's open feed** can let flame and smoke out when the draft fails (usual reasoning; S25 on spillage); the machine is tended, and that is part of what the lab says.
- **Burns**: the drum, the lid; a bench too hot to sit on means a too-short duct (K20).
- **Smoke-back**: at cold starts, by builders' own accounts (S49), and the critics' main point (S25, « high risk of spillage »); S23 gives the chain by which a depressed house reverses a stove after the fire. **The drum's lid leaks smoke on its first burns after opening, by design** (K63).
- **Weight**: about 3.4 t, a slab is not optional (K49, K13). A floor that settles cracks the casing, and a crack in the casing is a leak (usual reasoning).
- **The drum itself**: a surplus drum's paint and contents burn off, and solvent or oil residues are dangerous to open with heat (usual knowledge). The French video shows a recovered oil drum (S2). The lab says « cleaned of what it held », and that a used drum is a hazard in itself. **Galvanised steel** in a fire gives off zinc fumes (usual knowledge, not verified here).
- **Materials**: ordinary refractory ceramic fibre is classified as a possible carcinogen in the EU (from memory, **not verified**); the maker of the low-biopersistent fibre says its own is exonerated (K46). Dust from sawn firebrick: respirable crystalline silica, EU limit 0.1 mg/m³ (from memory, **not verified**).
- **Air pollution**: a J-tube burning badly smokes like any stove. Bans exist (§7.3).
- **The tin-can « rocket » of the wrong video** (S3): a flueless stove in a van or a room is the plainest way to die of CO. The lab draws the line in one sentence where the words « rocket stove » are defined: a rocket cook stove belongs outdoors; a mass heater has a chimney. Both share a name (S23 p. 11).
