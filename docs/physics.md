# Physics of the Mass Heater lab

The simulation in `src/sim` (pure TypeScript, no DOM, no three.js, runs in Node under vitest). It models a class of machine, never anyone's product and never a plan: a 200 mm J-tube with a drum, about 9 m of duct buried in about 3 t of cob, and a chimney, in a small well-insulated house (research §0.1).

The draft, the flow, the split of the heat, the exhaust, the room and the efficiency are **outcomes** of these equations. **Nothing is tuned to a reported figure.** Nobody has published a delivered efficiency of a cob J-tube, and any figure above 80 % is a flue calculation (research §2.3): a match here proves little, a miss is a finding. One input decides nearly everything and no source gives it: §1.3 and §6 say which, and how much.

Words used for a number: **reported** (a source of `docs/research.md`, K or S number), **derived** (computed from reported figures), **estimate** (the sources are silent: a choice with its reason), **usual** (a textbook value), **model** (an output of this simulation). In the data shown to a visitor `†` marks a model result and `*` an estimate.

## 1. The model

All SI, temperatures in °C. Files: `machine.ts` (every dimension and constant, its tag: `src/three/dims.ts` imports from here), `combustion.ts` (wood, air, dew point, CO), `plant.ts` (the machine at one instant and one step), `runs.ts` (a settled day, the wood a house needs, the claims), `presets.ts`, `types.ts`, `engine.ts` (`MassHeaterSim implements LabSim`), `texts.ts`. The reusable core is `thermal/` (§2).

### 1.1 One network

Everything that holds heat is a node of one RC network with enthalpy nodes (the core's `createNetwork`, `stepNetwork` with stable substeps): 3 layers of stone wall, the room's surfaces, its air, fireclay around the fire (150 kg), earth around that (250 kg), two layers of riser insulation, a steel sleeve, the drum's lid, upper and lower wall, the flue indoors and outdoors, a slab under the core and under each slice of bench, and the bench (§1.5). 138 nodes, 356 links. A step costs **about 22 µs** here. The gases hold next to nothing: at each step the flow and their temperatures are found on the present solids, and what they give up is applied to the solids as a heat. Energy closes to rounding over a day: a test holds it to 1e-6 of the wood, in every preset, including a reversal.

### 1.2 The fire

Wood goes in at `feedKgH` (K6) for `sessionHours` (K5), rising over 10 minutes, or as a closed load on a half-sine over `burnMinutes` (K29). Its energy, `Hu(M)` by K37 (which already pays for the water's latent heat: not subtracted twice), goes into two pools: volatiles, 70 %, time constant 5 min; char, 30 %, time constant 40 min (estimate: 15 to 25 % of the dry mass at 30 MJ/kg). A fire short of air releases only 3.0 MJ per kg of air (K35, K37). Phases: out, catching, burning, burning down, embers. Ash 0.77 % of the wood (K12).

### 1.3 The gas path and the draft

A chain from the feed to the cap: cover and sticks, tunnel, riser, gap, fall down the drum, manifold, six slices of duct with three bends, tee, flue indoors, flue outdoors, cap.

- **The flow** comes from `Σ g·(∫ρ_ref dz − ρ_i Δz_i) − p_fan = Σ K_i·ṁ²/(2ρ_i A_i²)`, solved by `solveLoopFlow`: walk from the last flow the way the pressure pushes, then a false position. **It has a sign, and reversal is an answer**: a warm path and a cold one can both stand, and the machine stays on its branch until it stops existing.
- **The reference column** is the room's air up to the height where the house's leaks balance (`neutralZ`, estimate: mid-height, 1.25 m) and the outside air above it. This moves little (§6), but it is why a cold flue pulls less than the hand sum.
- **Gas properties.** Air's, corrected for what the wood adds: molar mass, and a cₚ by mass of air, CO₂ and steam (the research's 5 % check: at λ = 2, cₚ is 6 % above air's, so the correction is in). One cₚ per step over the whole march, read at the mean temperature: energy closes exactly, and the error on a local temperature is under 4 %.
- **Losses**, in the velocity head of each section and density, all from usual tables, all estimates: tunnel 1.1, riser foot 1.1, gap 1.5 + (A/A_gap)², annulus 1.0, manifold 1.5, bends 0.9 each, tee 1.3, cap 1.0.
- **THE input: the feed.** A cover in cold air, K = 2.7 on its opening, then the sticks, K = 1.5 on the room they leave (50 %, estimate from the volume of a load), at the density of the neck. **No source gives the opening.** The first run left the feed open: **λ = 12, a 250 °C chamber, 52 %**, a machine nobody describes. The frames seen of real builds show bricks on the feed (research §3). From that alone: one brick of 22 × 11 cm laid flat hides 11 of the 18 cm, the sticks stand in the remaining slot, about 40 % of it free: **50 cm² of 324, `feedCover` = 0.85**. I set it there and did not move it after. A check that was not used to set it: the same opening on a 150 mm bore at 2 kg/h gives **6.9 g/s at 42 °C** against K11's eyeballed 6 g/s at 40 °C. That is one match on one eyeballed figure.
- **Spill.** Smoke leaves by the feed when the pull across it is less than a quarter of the weight difference of a hot feed column (estimate); on a reversed flow, always.
- **On a reversed flow** the fire pushes up the feed: 15 % of its heat reaches the brick, 15 % the riser's foot, the rest the room (estimates, **the least sure numbers here**). Nobody feeds a fire that smokes at them: the feed falls to a fifth.
- **Heat.** A film of forced and natural convection together, Nu = (Nu_f³ + Nu_n³)^(1/3), plus the gas's own radiation (ε = 0.06, Hottel's charts for a lean gas at this path length). A jet on the lid. The flame on the brick with ε = 0.3. Radiation from the sleeve to the drum, and from the drum to the room's surfaces, is read at each step.
- **The transition, stated plainly.** `tubeFlow` is laminar below Re 2,300 and turbulent above, and both of its laws jump there (Nu from 3.66 to about 7, f from 0.028 to 0.05). Nothing in the core smooths this. The duct runs at Re about 4,000 at full fire and falls through the jump as the fire falls, so this is where it lives. Here the natural film smooths the heat: `Nu_n` with a coefficient 0.06 (0.1 on a wall, usual, less for a cooled horizontal tube, which stratifies) gives about 2.5 W/m²·K at 100 K, as much as the forced film. **With the gas's radiation the duct's film is about 4 W/m²·K, twice the research's 1.8.** This one coefficient has a slider (`ductFilm`): × 0.5 gives 111 °C and 69 %, × 2 gives 51 °C and 85 %.
- **Dew point and condensation.** Gas composition from the wood (K35), Magnus's law. K41 is retrieved to a degree. Water counted when the exhaust falls under it.
- **The pressure along the path** is on `state.pathP`, against the air beside it, for the draft budget sheet and the world's strip. **Under the drum's lid it is −8.9 Pa at full burn in the default, −0.7 Pa on a 2.7 m chimney**: why a low chimney is a threat even where its efficiency looks better. A leak there is a hole with that pressure across it (`leakCm2`).

### 1.4 Carbon monoxide: an indication, never a measure

**No validated model predicts ppm here.** A map, its shape chosen to sit between the few readings, reports a **regime first** (none, hot flame, much air, cold chamber, short of air, wet wood, embers, smoke coming back) and a **band of a factor of three each way** second. Tests hold the ordering of the regimes, never their values.

- In flame: 2 g CO per kg of wood in a hot chamber (K9's 540 °C), climbing as (1 + (540 − T)/60)^1.5 under it, as exp(6·(1.4 − λ)) under λ = 1.4 (K36), and with moisture over 20 %. Capped at 150 g/kg.
- In char: a share r of its carbon leaves as CO: 0.25 under 150 °C, falling exponentially with a 90 K scale above it, floor 0.003. So embers over a cooling chamber are its worst moment, as K20 reports for the end of a load.
- **What it gives:** about 230 ppm at the lean default (band 80 to 700), 170 ppm at 5 kg/h and a hot chamber, 850 ppm on wet wood, 2,000 ppm as the load burns out. K19's mean is 312, K20's hot burn 20 to 100, K22's order of magnitude 800 to 3,000. **1,470 mg/MJ over a day** (22 g/kg), at the top of the table's modern stoves (765 to 1,530), six times K24's measured 240. That measured heater had a door, a grate, a laboratory. It is not a flattering number and it is not a prediction.
- **In the room:** one well-mixed volume of 185 m³ with its air change, the fire's CO entering by the spill and by the leak. K59's sum is retrieved within 4 %. The running means against WHO's four values (K60) and an alarm's dose at the standard's latest times (K61) are computed from it. **The whole house as one zone: a real room, or a sleeping person beside a leak, can be far from that mean.**

**The alarms of this lab are honest about what they are:** they say what the model sees going wrong. Their absence says nothing of a real installation, and the panel says so once.

### 1.5 The bench

Six slices along the duct, each an `xy` grid of 5 × 4 cells less the duct's own, 19 nodes. The duct is a square of the same area (the mass of K49 comes out exactly, a test holds it), giving up heat to its four neighbours through its film and their half-cell, on a share of the round duct's real area. The duct's steel holds next to nothing and is left out. The seat's cover is thick on the first 3 m and thin beyond, both from the draft (K13), the duct's axis 5 cm higher on the thin run, so that the top stays flat and the mass holds. Top and front go to the room through their half-cell, a film of natural convection and radiation (split between air and surfaces); back through the wall to outside; bottom through the slab and the floor's insulation to the ground at 8 °C. Conduction along the bench is left out: under 2 % of what crosses it, tested. A second grid is not needed: a 2-cell cover puts the peak within 5 % of a 24-cell one (`skinResponse`).

### 1.6 Room and house

One zone of 74 m² and 185 m³. Nodes: air, surfaces (60 kJ/m²·K of floor: furniture and plaster, estimate), and the inner stone wall in 3 layers (K, research §3). H in W/K from the surfaces to the outside, the base air change of 0.3 per hour inside it. The chimney's draw adds to the air change in quadrature (the usual rule; S27 calls it real). An extract fan adds 30 m³/h per Pa. Gains 220 W, about 3 W/m² (estimate: people, appliances, low sun). Outside a local function: **a Geneva January, mean 2.1 °C (S42, to check), ±3 K, warmest at 15:00** (assumption). There is no `sim/climate` in the kit.

### 1.7 Clocks, the day, the slider

- **Two clocks** (film contract): `step(dt)` moves the flame's clock and what is drawn at `dt × slowMo`; the hour, the fire's fuel, the bench and the room at `dt × timeScale`, with steps of at most 20 s burning, 90 s at rest. With `timeScale` 0 the bench stops and the flame runs on. No timer, no wall clock, no random number.
- **A settled day.** `settledDay(params)` runs days until one ends where it began: from a first guess, then each day the whole house is lifted by the stock it gained over what its H would lose (a Newton step on its one slow mode), 3 to 7 days, about 140 ms here, cached by settings, deterministic. Only the last day counts. Steps of 60 and 240 s; a finer step moves its figures by under a point.
- **The slider.** 96 frames hold the whole machine. `set({ hour })` restores one, and live play carries on from it. A day played live ends within 0.6 K of the table. A frame keeps the fire's pools with the last step's feed in, as that step's fire read them: the slider and the charts read the same fire (see the correction below).
- **Correction by the lead, 2026-11-02 (integration).** The frames used to keep the bare pools, after the step's burn and before the next step's feed. A frame brought back read a fire short of one step's wood: at 09:00, **8.7 kW** where the feed gives 10.4 kW and the day's own series 10.2 kW, so a chamber of **434 °C** where the series says **485 °C**, with the mean of a fire at full rate at **467 °C**. **485 °C is the state at 09:00, 467 °C the mean, and 434 °C was the error.** The settled day's series, summaries and shares were never touched: only what was read after `set({ hour })`. The figures read that way move, and are corrected in this file: at 09:00 riser 5.7 → 6.0 Pa, fall −4.5 → −4.7 Pa, net 1.2 → 1.3 Pa, chimney 14.7 → 14.9 Pa, lid −8.8 → −8.9 Pa (−0.8 → −0.7 Pa on the low chimney), top of the riser 414 → 459 °C, after the drum 239 → 249 °C, dew point 27 → 29 °C; the CO of the default 380 → 230 ppm (band 130–1,100 → 80–700) and of wet wood 1,080 → 850 ppm. **The CO map's shape was not touched**: it was chosen while the default read 380 ppm, and that default now sits under K19's 312, which the owner and the physics role may want to weigh. The damper and the fan start after the feed has stopped: their figures do not move.
- **Moments for a film**, numbers on `state`: `fireCount`, `burning`, `flowDirection`, `coAlarm`, `benchPeakHour`. Gestures: `lightFire()`, `addLoad()`, `closeLid()`, `openLid()`, `warmFlue()`, `applyPreset(id)`, `applyScenario(id)`, `reset()`. Params: `hour`, `thermalView`, `feedKgH`.

### 5.3 Schumack's four directions (K42)

By sign, and his numbers were not reached. **A partial damper and a longer duct raise the efficiency:** found, both by less air or more surface. **A low chimney:** he finds a threat to performance; here the efficiency rises, by the same mechanism as a damper, while the margin under the lid falls from −8.9 to −0.7 Pa. His model may have held the burn rate differently. **A small clearance above the barrel's riser:** he finds a threat; here, at 1.5 cm and these flows, little, because the velocity head at the gap is a fraction of a pascal. At 1 cm and 5 kg/h, still little. Either the model misses what a narrow gap does to a flame, or his case burns far harder. I cannot tell without his paper (Q9 of the research).

## 7. Assumptions and limits

**To confirm, by weight:**

1. **The feed's opening: 50 cm² of 324, `feedCover` 0.85.** One brick and a slot, from frames seen. Decides λ, the chamber, the efficiency. One O₂ reading on a real steady J-tube would replace it (S18's author logged CO and may have O₂; research Q9 could ask).
2. **The house:** H 106 W/K, 220 W of gains, one zone, 60 kJ/m²·K plus the stone wall, 0.3 air change. All assumptions. **The room moves 6.7 K a day where K17 reports 1 to 2 K overnight.** A heavier house would move less; it would not change the wood.
3. **The duct's film:** natural coefficient 0.06, ε = 0.06. Decides the exhaust.
4. **The chimney:** 5 m, 3 cm of insulation at 0.06 W/m·K. Its draft is well founded; its height is not.
5. **Char:** 30 % of the energy, 40 min. Sets how long the embers last, so when a lid may go on, and the CO at the end.
6. **`neutralZ`** 1.25 m; a tight house with a low air inlet sits lower, one with attic leaks higher. It moves a cold flue from −1.1 to −2.4 Pa, a burn hardly.
7. **Reversal:** the 15 % + 15 % of a fire's heat, the fifth of its feed, the quarter column for spill, 4 g/s of exchange. Estimates, with no data at all.
8. **Materials:** cob's 1,700 kg/m³ (research, estimate); core's 150 + 250 kg (estimate); the lid's, drum's and earth's emissivities (usual).
9. **The CO map.** §1.4.
10. **Sticks and loads:** the feed is a steady rate, not a load every half hour. A real session breathes.

**Limits of the model.**

- **It cannot say an installation is safe, and it does not try.** No crack, no settling floor, no wind on the cap, no blocked flue, no soot, no chimney fire, no creosote, no failed joint but the one leak at the lid. Room CO is a whole-house mean.
- Well-mixed gas at each station: a flame's own temperature is not computed. A burn rate that does not answer to the air: a real J-tube burns faster when it breathes more, which this model only sees as more λ.
- Moisture of the cob, a first drying, a real first week: none. k and c constant in cob. No thermal mass of duct steel. Bends add no heat exchange.
- Ground at a fixed 8 °C. No sun on the house but its share of the gains. One climate, one wave.
- `yurt` and `batch-load` keep the same room, drum and slab as the machine.

**What the lead asked, plainly.** J-tube with a batch box as a preset, no closing damper, cob, a generic house, the claims said not to balance: **none of these changes the model**, and the model agrees with the last one. One thing the plan's default leaves open: at 2.5 kg/h a 200 mm path is a slow fire, so the opening decides much; 12.5 kg in shorter, faster sessions gives the same day with a hotter chamber (704 °C at 5 kg/h, same 78 to 80 %).

**This lab explains; it does not size.** Nothing in the panel reads as a design answer: no « OK », no recommended value, no safe range, the sliders are said once to be no size to build. `docs/physics.md` holds the proportions as the sources' words.
